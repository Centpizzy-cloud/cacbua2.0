# CACBUA SEO Strategy

**Status:** Planning only — no SEO implementation or deployment authorized

**Prepared:** 13 September 2026

**Starting point:** Approved CACBUA redesign, its verified event archive, and Publication 001: *China's Zero-Tariff Policy for Africa: What the 2026 Expansion Means for African Exporters*

## Executive principles

CACBUA should become a trusted search destination for people trying to understand and act on China–Africa trade, investment, policy, market access, and institutional cooperation. Search visibility must be earned through accurate, useful, sourced material—not generic volume.

Every publishable page must identify its content class:

- **CACBUA CONTENT:** CACBUA's verified identity, services, leadership direction, approved activities, event archive, institutional analysis, and documented first-party experience.
- **EXTERNAL RESEARCH:** Policy facts, trade statistics, market developments, and third-party reporting, with visible sources and dates. External developments must never imply CACBUA participation.
- **REVIEW REQUIRED:** Claims, biographies, partnerships, country guidance, or redirect decisions that lack sufficient evidence or institutional approval.

No unverified CACBUA achievement, client outcome, partner relationship, investment facilitated, or event participation should be created for SEO purposes.

- Homepage → About, Research, Cooperation, Opportunities, News, Events, Leadership, Contact.
- Publication → parent Research hub, relevant topic hub, country page, Opportunities section, and a contextual cooperation/contact CTA.
- Country page → relevant publications, opportunities, events, and Cooperation.
- Event detail → related research topic, relevant country, and event inquiry.
- News article → one evergreen hub plus one related publication/event when genuinely relevant.
- Founder → About, Cooperation, Research, Contact.
- Opportunities → source-backed publication where available; otherwise Contact with the opportunity topic carried into the inquiry.
- Breadcrumbs on all pages below the top level.
- Add `Related research`, `Related opportunities`, or `Related events` modules only where editorially relevant; do not automate irrelevant links.
- Run an orphan-page crawl before launch. Every indexable page should receive at least one internal link.

## 8. China–Africa country-page strategy

### Purpose

Country pages should help a decision-maker understand a specific African country's economic relationship with China and discover relevant CACBUA research—not exist merely to capture `[country] + China` keywords.

### Minimum publication standard

A country page requires:

1. Scope and last-reviewed date.
2. Bilateral trade overview from named primary sources.
3. Major sectors, policies, and market-access considerations.
4. Verified institutional context.
5. Current opportunities and limitations.
6. Related CACBUA publications/events.
7. Sources and correction contact.
8. Visible labeling of **CACBUA CONTENT** versus **EXTERNAL RESEARCH**.

Do not launch 54 thin pages. Pilot Nigeria because the redesign and Publication 001 already contain relevant sourced material. Add countries only when a named editor can maintain them. Country pages should be updated at least annually and whenever a material policy change occurs.

**Pilot proposed URL:** `/countries/nigeria/china-trade-investment/`

**Status:** REVIEW REQUIRED before creation; confirm scope, primary sources, update ownership, and whether CACBUA has country-specific first-party material.

If Chinese-language content becomes available, use a deliberate `/zh/` structure with equivalent content and reciprocal `hreflang` (`en`, `zh-Hans`, and `x-default`)—not automatic or partial machine translation. **REVIEW REQUIRED.**

## 9. Research/publication SEO template

Each approved publication should contain:

- SEO title: `{Specific finding or question} | CACBUA Research`
- Meta description: 140–160-character plain-language benefit and scope.
- One H1 matching the actual paper topic.
- Content label: `CACBUA Analysis`, `External Research Summary`, or a precise mixed classification.
- Named author and affiliation; reviewer/approval owner.
- Publication and last-updated dates.
- Executive summary and key takeaways.
- Table of contents for long pieces.
- Clear research question, scope, and limitations.
- Sourced facts with direct links to primary material where possible.
- Visually separate CACBUA interpretation from external facts.
- Descriptive subheadings aligned to reader questions, not keyword repetition.
- Related country/topic/opportunity links.
- Contact CTA appropriate to the subject.
- Citation suggestion and downloadable PDF only if maintained alongside the HTML canonical.
- `Article` or `Report`-appropriate structured data (see schema section).
- Editorial status: draft pages remain `noindex` and out of the sitemap until approved.

## 10. News article SEO template

- URL: `/news/{descriptive-slug}/`
- SEO title: `{Clear event or development headline} | CACBUA`
- Meta description: concise subject, location/context, and reader value.
- H1, category, named author/editor, published and modified dates.
- Dateline only where accurate.
- Opening paragraph answers what happened, when, where, who, and why it matters.
- `CACBUA News` label only for verified CACBUA activity; otherwise `External Insight`.
- State CACBUA's role precisely: organizer, co-organizer, participant, supporter, or no involvement.
- Original sources and corrections contact.
- Captioned, licensed image with people/event identification where known.
- Related event, research, and cooperation links.
- `NewsArticle` schema only for genuine news pages.
- Do not change published dates merely to make old stories appear fresh; expose `dateModified` for substantive updates.

## 11. Image SEO rules

- Use only licensed/approved imagery recorded in `docs/image-sources.md`.
- Use descriptive filenames, e.g. `cacbua-mex-export-medical-equipment-expo-2023.webp`.
- Use standard `<img>` or `<picture>` markup; Google notes that CSS background images are not indexed like normal HTML images.
- Write concise alt text describing the image's purpose and visible content. Do not stuff keywords.
- Use empty `alt=""` for decorative images/icons.
- Add factual captions for event/editorial images where useful.
- Serve responsive `srcset`/`sizes`, modern WebP or AVIF plus fallback where needed.
- Set intrinsic `width` and `height` to reduce layout shift.
- Compress without visible degradation; avoid shipping original multi-megabyte files to article cards.
- Keep important text as HTML, not embedded in images.
- Add image entries to sitemaps for editorial images when helpful; verify any separate image-CDN domain in Search Console.
- Never reuse an image in a way that implies an incorrect country, event, organization, or CACBUA involvement.

Reference: [Google Image SEO best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images).

## 12. Schema recommendations

Implement JSON-LD only when visible page content supports it. Validate with Google's Rich Results Test and Schema.org validator.

- Sitewide homepage/About: `Organization` with canonical name, URL, logo, verified contact points, and only confirmed `sameAs` profiles. Do not add unverified founding date, awards, partners, or areas served.
- Sitewide: `WebSite`; add `SearchAction` only after real site search exists.
- Interior pages: `BreadcrumbList` matching visible breadcrumbs.
- Publications: `Article` or `Report` (Schema.org) with headline, description, author, publisher, dates, canonical URL, image if licensed, and subject keywords. Rich-result eligibility can vary; accuracy remains the purpose.
- News: `NewsArticle` with accurate author/date fields.
- Events: `Event` only for individual events with confirmed name, start date, location/online mode, status, organizer, and URL. Historical archives should use past dates accurately. Do not add offers unless tickets actually exist.
- Founder: `Person` connected to the `Organization` via `worksFor`/role where supported, using only approved biography facts.
- Contact: organization `ContactPoint`; avoid unsupported `LocalBusiness` claims unless CACBUA's legal/entity fit and physical-office eligibility are confirmed. **REVIEW REQUIRED.**
- FAQ schema: use only for genuine visible FAQs and check current Google eligibility before implementation; do not create generic FAQ blocks just for schema.

## 13. XML sitemap requirements

- Publish a sitemap index at `/sitemap_index.xml` or `/sitemap.xml` containing canonical, indexable, HTTP 200 URLs only.
- Exclude redirects, `noindex` pages, drafts, parameter URLs, search results, duplicate archives, and broken URLs.
- Use accurate `<lastmod>` dates reflecting substantive changes.
- Split by type when scale requires it: pages, publications, news, events, countries, images.
- Keep each sitemap within protocol limits and UTF-8 encoded.
- Reference the sitemap in `robots.txt` and submit it in Search Console.
- Test fetchability and syntax after deployment; monitor processing errors.
- Current Yoast sitemap inventory must be reconciled against the final canonical list before launch.

Google explains that sitemap submission supports discovery and lets owners monitor fetch/parse errors in Search Console: [Sitemaps report guidance](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7451001).

## 14. robots.txt requirements

Proposed minimum production file:

```text
User-agent: *
Disallow:

Sitemap: https://cacbua.org/sitemap_index.xml
```

- Do not block CSS, JavaScript, or image assets needed to render pages.
- Block crawl traps or private technical paths only after confirming they are unnecessary to render/index content.
- Do not use `robots.txt` as a substitute for `noindex`; Google explicitly distinguishes crawling controls from indexing controls.
- Staging should be password-protected and `noindex`; do not rely only on robots blocking.
- **Launch gate:** remove the current sitewide `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, follow`, then confirm no template meta `noindex` remains on approved public pages.

Reference: [Google crawling and indexing guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing).

## 15. Canonical URL rules

- Choose `https://cacbua.org/` as the single host/protocol form unless the infrastructure decision changes. Redirect HTTP and `www` alternatives to it.
- Every indexable HTML page gets one self-referencing absolute canonical.
- Canonicals must match sitemap URLs and internal links.
- Strip or canonicalize tracking parameters such as `utm_*`; do not canonicalize materially different content to an unrelated page.
- Paginated archives require intentional handling; do not canonicalize every page to page one if content differs.
- HTML publication is canonical when a duplicate PDF exists; use an HTTP `Link: <...>; rel="canonical"` header for the PDF if supported.
- Do not combine `noindex` with canonical as a routine duplicate-control method.
- Redirect eliminated duplicates server-side with permanent HTTP redirects.

Reference: [Google canonical URL guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls).

## 17. Core Web Vitals and performance checklist

Use field data where available and lab testing for diagnosis. Targets should meet Google's current “good” thresholds at the 75th percentile:

- LCP: ≤2.5 seconds.
- INP: ≤200 milliseconds.
- CLS: ≤0.1.

Checklist:

- Optimize the homepage hero and likely LCP image; responsive sizes, modern formats, correct preload only for the true LCP asset.
- Never lazy-load the above-the-fold LCP image; lazy-load below-fold images.
- Include image dimensions/aspect ratios.
- Minify and cache CSS/JS; remove unused rules/scripts and avoid render-blocking third-party code.
- Use long-lived immutable caching for versioned static assets.
- Compress HTML/CSS/JS with Brotli or gzip.
- Use a CDN where operationally appropriate; test origin and edge cache behavior.
- Self-host or efficiently load fonts; use `font-display: swap`; minimize families and weights.
- Keep interaction JavaScript small; test mobile navigation, search, newsletter, and analytics overhead.
- Avoid layout shifts from headers, embeds, consent banners, and dynamic cards.
- Set a performance budget: suggested ≤1.5 MB initial mobile page weight, ≤150 KB compressed JS, and minimal third-party scripts. **REVIEW REQUIRED** against the final platform.
- Test representative templates at mobile and desktop widths with PageSpeed Insights/Lighthouse and real-device throttling.
- Monitor Search Console Core Web Vitals after launch; fix template-wide problems before isolated low-traffic pages.
- Verify HTTP/2 or HTTP/3, TLS, redirects, 404 behavior, and time to first byte.

### SEO AT LAUNCH

1. Deploy approved site and one-to-one redirects; no redirect chains.
2. Remove sitewide `noindex` only from approved production content.
3. Verify HTTP 200/3xx/404 responses, canonicals, robots directives, and rendered content.
4. Confirm `robots.txt` and sitemap are publicly fetchable.
5. Activate validated analytics/consent configuration without duplicate tags.
6. Submit sitemap in the DNS-verified Search Console Domain property.
7. Inspect homepage and every core template; request indexing for a small set of priority URLs.
8. Crawl the live site for broken links, orphan pages, redirect errors, duplicate metadata, and accidental indexation.
9. Record launch benchmarks and exact deployed redirect rules.

### SEO FIRST 30 DAYS

1. Monitor indexing, sitemap processing, canonical selection, crawl errors, security/manual-action reports, and Core Web Vitals.
2. Check old URLs and backlinks against the redirect map; correct 404s and chains.
3. Review real search queries and refine page targeting without rewriting pages around irrelevant keywords.
4. Confirm analytics events and key events with no personal data leakage.
5. Improve internal links based on early impressions and user paths.
6. Publish no more than two thoroughly reviewed priority pieces—recommended: approved Publication 001 and the Nigeria country pilot or export-readiness guide.
7. Establish editorial owners, fact-checking, correction, review, and update schedules.

### SEO MONTHS 2–3

1. Publish two to four additional source-led pieces from the priority list, subject to review capacity.
2. Expand the strongest-performing topic cluster rather than distributing effort across every category.
3. Create individual event pages only for events with sufficient verified material.
4. Evaluate the Nigeria country pilot before approving more country pages.
5. Seek relevant institutional citations and links through genuine partnerships, expert contributions, event documentation, and publication outreach—not bulk link schemes.
6. Refresh weak titles/descriptions using Search Console evidence; strengthen pages with impressions but low click-through or low engagement.
7. Run a 90-day technical crawl, content-quality review, Core Web Vitals review, redirect audit, and analytics/privacy audit.
8. Present an evidence-based next-quarter editorial plan to CACBUA leadership.

## Decision gates

The following require explicit CACBUA approval before implementation or publication:

- Final URL structure and all redirects.
- Removal of production `noindex` directives.
- Publication 001 institutional status.
- Founder biography and any expanded leadership facts.
- Country-page pilot scope and update ownership.
- Chinese-language architecture and translation workflow.
- Analytics/consent/privacy configuration.
- Schema claims about legal entity type, founding details, service area, partners, or achievements.

This strategy stops at planning. It does not authorize metadata changes, URL renames, redirects, schema deployment, analytics installation, Search Console changes, content publication, or production deployment.
